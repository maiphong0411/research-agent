## Problem

"most existing benchmarks judge only final outcomes, unable to distinguish
reliable reasoning from lucky success or attribute failures to specific process
deficiencies, hindering attribution in long-horizon tasks."

## Method

Dual-assessment benchmark: a **Task Score** ("what an agent achieves") and a
**Process Score** ("how it achieves it"). A Process Grader scores each reasoning
turn on four dimensions — "goal alignment, efficiency, information utilization,
and result verification".

## Results

- **320** tasks across **8** domains with "25+ deterministic mock services".
- **12,541** task-specific rubric items anchor the Process Grader.
- **21** models over **16,000+** trials.
- Claims: process scores filter "lucky passes invisible to outcome-only
  evaluation"; the four dimensions are complementary "with **result verification**
  as the systematic bottleneck"; the framework is "robust to evaluator choice
  across different judge LLMs"; and "process-based trajectory filtering yields
  consistent post-training improvements across model scales".

## Failure modes

Abstract-only — these are inferences from the abstract, not from the experiments.

- `[observed]` **12,541 hand-anchored rubric items over 320 tasks is ~39 items per
  task, and their provenance decides everything.** Whether they were written by
  humans, generated by a model, or generated and reviewed is not stated in the
  abstract. If model-generated, the benchmark inherits the phantom-criteria problem
  that [[rosset2026artbuildingverifierscomputer]] identifies as the root
  pathology of rubric verifiers ("requirements never stated in the task…
  inflating the denominator"). Checking rubric provenance is the first thing to
  read the paper for.

- `[observed]` **"Robust to evaluator choice across different judge LLMs" is a
  weaker claim than it reads.** Agreement among LLM judges is consistency, not
  correctness; a shared bias produces exactly this result. No human agreement
  figure appears in the abstract, which is the number that would distinguish the
  two. Contrast [[fan2026agentprocessbenchdiagnosingsteplevelprocess]], which
  reports 89.1% human IAA and Cohen's κ 0.767.

- `[observed]` **"25+ deterministic mock services" buys reproducibility at the cost
  of the failure modes that matter.** Deterministic mocks remove the environment
  non-determinism, flaky tools, and partial failures that real deployments exhibit
  — and the paper's own headline bottleneck (result verification) is precisely the
  capability that only matters when the environment can surprise the agent.

- `[observed]` **The post-training result is where reward and evaluation touch.**
  "process-based trajectory filtering yields consistent post-training
  improvements" — filtering training data with the same grader that scores the
  benchmark is the correlated-measurement-bias pattern
  [[chen2026horizongapplanningmemory]] names as an open problem. Whether the
  post-training gain is measured on Task Score (independent) or Process Score
  (circular) is not stated.

## Relevance

Directly on open problem 3 (`wiki/agents/open-problems.md`): "distinguish reliable
reasoning from lucky success" is that entry's thesis in the authors' own words, at
320 tasks and 21 models. Its "result verification as the systematic bottleneck"
finding is the interesting one for open problem 2 — if agents systematically fail
to check their own results, they cannot detect the state that mid-trajectory
repair would act on. Worth a full read if that finding survives inspection.
