## Problem

Step-level verification benchmarks are "predominantly confined to closed-world
mathematical domains", while "standard agent benchmarks such as GAIA and τ²-Bench
only report end-to-end task success, and do not provide step-level signals for
evaluating PRMs."

The motivating asymmetry: "unlike mathematical reasoning where errors can often be
rectified via backtracking, tool execution frequently entails irreversible side
effects—such as sending erroneous emails or deleting essential files."

## Method

Given a task `T` and trajectory `X`, label every assistant step with
`y_i ∈ {−1, 0, +1}`:

- **+1** "factually correct and clearly advances task completion", including
  "identifying an error in a preceding step and taking an appropriate corrective
  action".
- **0** "neutral or exploratory" — "reasonable but yields limited or negligible
  impact", e.g. "a 404 error from a valid URL", redundant restatement.
- **−1** "incorrect or harmful", including "violating policy constraints or
  repeating failed actions without a substantive change in strategy".

**Error-propagation rule:** "once an erroneous step occurs, all subsequent steps
that depend on or are causally related to this mistake are labeled as −1 until the
agent explicitly corrects the error or transitions to a new subtask that is
independent of the earlier failure."

Two metrics: **StepAcc** = micro-averaged label agreement, all steps pooled so
"longer trajectories contributing proportionally more steps"; **FirstErrAcc** =
fraction of trajectories where the predicted first-`−1` index matches the human
one, and "If neither prediction nor reference contains a −1 label, the trajectory
is considered error-free and counted as correct."

Data: 200 unique tasks evenly sampled from HotpotQA, GAIA, BFCL, τ²-Bench;
trajectories rolled out from five source models (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507,
Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, DeepSeek-V3.2, GPT-5-mini, GPT-5), "For every
selected task, we preserve trajectories generated by all five models."

## Results

- **1,000** trajectories, **8,509** annotated agent actions, inter-annotator
  agreement **89.1%**, Cohen's κ **0.767**. 20 LLMs evaluated.
- Annotators were given "reference annotations generated by three
  state-of-the-art LLMs: DeepSeek-V3.2, GPT-5.2, and Claude 4.5 Sonnet". Final
  human labels agree with those three references "only from **66.9% to 72.1%**".
- Best overall: Gemini-3-Flash-Preview-Thinking, average StepAcc **81.6%** but
  FirstErrAcc only **65.8%**. Best open-source: Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 at
  **68.5%** StepAcc.
- StepAcc vs FirstErrAcc correlate at "average Pearson r = **0.90** and Spearman
  ρ = **0.92**", yet "FirstErrAcc is consistently lower than StepAcc".
- StepAcc vs trajectory-level final accuracy: "Pearson r = **0.814**,
  p = **1.2 × 10⁻⁵**".
- Complexity effect HotpotQA → GAIA: Gemini-3-Flash-Preview drops "2.9% and
  16.8%" (StepAcc, FirstErrAcc); Qwen3-4B "suffers much sharper declines of
  **22.8%** and **30%**".
- Reasoning mode: "Qwen3-8B in reasoning mode achieves a 6.1% higher StepAcc and
  a 5.3% higher FirstErrAcc than its non-reasoning variant." But
  "GPT-5.2-Chat markedly outperforms its thinking variant on multi-turn tool-use
  benchmarks (BFCL and τ²-bench)."
- Capability density: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 beats Qwen3-8B on FirstErrAcc,
  "**44.4%** vs. **40.7%**", at "only 50% of the parameters".
- Best-of-N (N=8) on GAIA: "majority-voting achieves **49.1%** accuracy, while
  the oracle Pass@N is **77.4%**". The two-stage (Final→Process) selector
  "consistently improves performance across all generators" over the outcome-only
  criterion.
- Error-propagation ablation (two-stage Best-of-8 on GAIA), Original → w/o EP:
  DeepSeek-V3.2 49.1 → 52.8 (**Δ +3.7**); DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking 64.2 → 50.9
  (**Δ −13.2**); Gemini-3-Flash-Preview 58.5 → 52.8 (**Δ −5.7**).
- GRPO on 800 annotated trajectories with StepAcc as reward: validation StepAcc
  "improves from **55.3%** to **74.6%**" on the held-out 200.
- Anchoring check: 100 trajectories re-annotated without model references by a
  separate group; "The step-level agreement with the original labels is
  **84.06%**, compared with the inter-annotator agreement of 89.1%".
- Neutral steps are rare across subsets (per-subset neutral step ratio runs from
  about 2.6% to 10.2%) but "consistently harder than positive and negative steps
  for both models"; both the strongest closed- and open-source models "exhibit a
  tendency to over-predict the positive (+1) label".

## Failure modes

- `[observed]` **The benchmark cannot support its own motivating claim.** The
  paper opens on irreversibility — "tool execution frequently entails irreversible
  side effects" — as the reason step-level verification matters more here than in
  math. But nothing in the label schema marks irreversibility. A formatting typo
  in a tool call and an irreversible `delete` both get `−1`. A PRM trained on
  these labels therefore learns "wrong", never "unrecoverable", which is exactly
  the distinction the introduction says the field needs. Adding a fourth label,
  or a binary reversibility flag on `−1`, is a well-scoped attack that the
  existing 8,509 annotations could be extended with.

- `[observed]` **StepAcc rewards ignoring the paper's own novel contribution.**
  The neutral label is the stated methodological advance ("we introduce a neutral
  label (0) to explicitly accommodate the exploratory nature of real-world
  agents"), it is the hardest class, and it is a small single-digit-to-~10% share
  of steps. Under a micro-averaged metric, a model that never predicts `0` forfeits
  almost nothing. So the headline ranking in Figure 1 is close to blind to
  exploration-awareness. Recomputing the leaderboard under macro-F1 or per-class
  recall would likely reorder it, and no such breakdown is reported.

- `[observed]` **Error propagation makes StepAcc count one mistake many times
  over.** By construction, all causally dependent steps after an error are `−1`,
  so a single early failure in a 16-step trajectory can generate a dozen labels.
  Since "All assistant steps across all trajectories are pooled together", long
  failed trajectories dominate the metric, and what StepAcc measures is partly
  "can the model tell it is downstream of a failure", not "can it detect a
  failure". The authors note FirstErrAcc is "less susceptible to error
  propagation" but still lead with StepAcc.

- `[observed]` **The annotation protocol is worth more than the model.** Table 5
  shows removing error propagation moves Best-of-8 by −13.2 for
  DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking but +3.7 for DeepSeek-V3.2 — a swing larger than most
  model-to-model gaps in Table 3, and in opposite directions for two variants of
  the *same* model. The paper's reading ("error propagation can benefit test-time
  scaling when the step evaluator is capable") is a post-hoc account of an n = 3
  ablation. Any downstream result built on this benchmark inherits a protocol
  choice with effect size comparable to the thing being measured.

- `[observed]` **Ground truth is anchored on the models being graded.**
  Annotators saw reference labels from DeepSeek-V3.2, GPT-5.2, and Claude 4.5
  Sonnet, and two of those families are then evaluated on the resulting benchmark.
  The control experiment shows reference-free re-annotation agrees only 84.06%
  against 89.1% IAA — the authors read this as references "reduce annotation
  difficulty", but it equally supports ~16% of labels moving when the model
  suggestion is removed. The reported 66.9–72.1% human-vs-reference agreement
  bounds the anchoring, it does not eliminate it.

- `[observed]` **FirstErrAcc scores quitting as success.** "If neither prediction
  nor reference contains a −1 label, the trajectory is considered error-free and
  counted as correct." Combined with the paper's own fail-fast finding — "although
  Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 exhibits the lowest trajectory-level success rate, it
  attains a relatively higher step-level accuracy … the model is more likely to
  terminate early" — the metric introduced to correct for fail-fast inherits a
  version of the same bug. An agent that emits one correct step and stops is
  error-free under both metrics.

- `[observed]` **200 tasks, not 1,000.** The 1,000 trajectories are five rollouts
  over the same 200 tasks, and tasks were selected "by maximizing pairwise
  embedding distance" *within* four source benchmarks. Steps from the same task
  share tool schema, environment state, and ground-truth solution, so the
  effective sample size behind 8,509 labels is much smaller than it reads, and no
  clustered standard errors are reported anywhere.

- `[observed]` **The test-time-scaling result never uses step localization.** The
  process signals in Best-of-N are "# Pos Step and % Pos Ratio" — scalar counts of
  positive labels. So the demonstration that "process-derived signals provide
  complementary value to outcome supervision" exercises the PRM as an aggregate
  quality scorer, not as an error localizer, which is what FirstErrAcc measures
  and what credit assignment needs. The gap between the best practical selector
  and oracle Pass@8 (77.4%) is therefore unattributed: it is unknown how much is
  PRM accuracy versus throwing away positional information.

- `[observed]` **"Thinking helps" does not hold on the tool-heavy subsets.**
  GPT-5.2-Chat beats GPT-5.2-Thinking on both multi-turn tool benchmarks, and the
  explanation offered is a hypothesis about "specialized optimizations for dialogue
  dynamics". Since BFCL and τ²-Bench are the two subsets closest to deployed
  agentic use, the headline claim that "thinking models significantly outperform
  their instruct counterparts" is weakest exactly where it would matter.

- `[stated]` **No GUI or computer-use agents.** Environments are Web + CLI + APIs;
  extension to "GUI-based agents and computer-using agents" is future work. Step
  granularity in a GUI setting (one click) differs enough from a tool call that
  the label schema may not transfer.

## Relevance

The most direct answer yet to open problem 3 in `wiki/agents/open-problems.md` —
τ-bench's terminal-state reward, which the τ-bench authors themselves called "a
necessary but not sufficient condition". This supplies human step labels over
τ²-Bench trajectories, so it is the missing ground truth for a trajectory-aware
reward. Two things it does *not* fix, and both are leads: it has no notion of
irreversibility despite that being its stated motivation, and it never uses error
*position* in any downstream experiment. Also relevant to open problem 2 — the +1
definition explicitly credits "identifying an error in a preceding step and
taking an appropriate corrective action", making this the first dataset here that
labels mid-trajectory repair at all.
